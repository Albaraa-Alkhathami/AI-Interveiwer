# Final Dataset — Limitations

**File:** `combined_dataset.csv`
**Snapshot:** 23,954 rows, 46 subjects, 3 difficulty levels, 138/138 subject×difficulty combinations covered.
**Schemas:** `prompt, question, answer, subject, difficulty, data_flag`
**Prepared:** 13 November 2026, updated 15 November 2026. All figures below were measured on this exact file.

Use this document whenever the dataset is used for training, evaluation, reporting or comparison. Several limitations are structural and cannot be fixed by sampling differently.

## 2. Provenance and lineage

1. The source combined file held **28,770 rows**.
2. **309 rows** were exact duplicates — every row from `qa_sheet.xlsx` already existed verbatim in the source, so the merge contributed nothing and those rows were dropped.
3. **4,931 rows** were repeat copies of the same question with a differently generated answer. One row per question was kept, preferring a non-empty answer that ends in proper punctuation, then the longest.
4. **11 rows** were authored by hand to close the first coverage gaps.
5. **104 questions** were generated by `openai/gpt-oss-20b` (Groq free tier) via `generate_questions.py`, deduplicated against the existing corpus at cosine > 0.90, to close the remaining gaps → 23,954.

Consequences of step 3 are covered in §4 and §5 and remain the most important limitation in this document.

## 3. Class imbalance — subjects

- The twelve industry subjects (Cybersecurity, AI & Data Platforms, Drones & Aerial Platforms, E-Commerce & Global Trade, HealthTech & BioTech, FinTech, Gaming, AdTech, DefenseTech, SaaS & Cloud Platforms, Logistics & Supply Chain, FoodTech) hold **85.4%** of all rows.
- The remaining 34 subjects share **14.6%**, and 7 of them have fewer than 30 questions.
- Two subjects still have fewer than 20 questions and are marked `limited data (<20 questions)` in the `data_flag` column:

| Subject | Questions |
|---|---:|
| General Computer Science | 12 |
| Version Control | 17 |

**Impact:** any per-subject statistic, model fine-tune or benchmark slice on these subjects is unreliable — 12 to 17 examples cannot represent a topic. Aggregate metrics are dominated by the twelve large subjects.

**Mitigation:** report per-subject metrics with sample sizes; exclude or pool the two flagged subjects; never compare subject accuracy without weighting.

## 4. Lost answer variants

The deduplication kept **one answer per question**. The removed copies were not duplicates of each other: the same question appeared up to 35 times, each with a differently worded answer.

**Impact:**
- The dataset is **not** a source of multiple acceptable answers per question. If you need answer diversity for preference ranking, evaluation rubrics or contrastive training, this file cannot provide it.
- The surviving answer is "most complete by length and punctuation", which is a crude proxy for best. It is not a quality judgement.
- Any question that would be answered differently in different contexts (e.g. by seniority) now carries only one viewpoint.

**Mitigation:** the pre-clean backup `combined_dataset_original_backup.csv` (28,770 rows) still holds every variant; use it if answer diversity matters.

## 5. Subject labels are single-label and partly arbitrary

When the same question existed under several subjects, only the retained row's subject survived.

**Impact:**
- 117 question texts originally spanned more than one subject; each now appears under exactly one.
- Some rows are labelled with a subject that was one of several valid contexts, so the subject column is **not** a multi-label topic map and is mildly noisy.
- Coverage figures per subject therefore understate true topical reach.

**Mitigation:** treat `subject` as a primary label, not an exhaustive classification. Re-label from question text if multi-label use is required.

## 6. 115 rows have different provenance

The **11 authored rows** (Personal Information ×2, Leadership & Teamwork ×2, Vue.js, LLM Fine-Tuning, DevOps, C++, Software Testing, CUDA, Version Control) and the **104 generated questions** were **not** produced by the original pipeline.

**Impact:** wording, length and structure follow the dataset's style but are not statistically comparable to the rest. They are 0.48% of rows, concentrated in exactly the sparse subjects and cells where they matter most.

**Mitigation:** the generated rows are listed verbatim in `questions_generated_all.csv`; filter on question text if strict provenance is required.

## 7. Difficulty labels are unvalidated

Difficulty is inherited from the source and was not re-checked against content.

- Hard is the smallest class (7,182 vs 8,112 Medium), but **every subject now carries all three levels** after the gap-filling pass.
- Labels appear to track question type rather than measured complexity (Easy ≈ definitional, Medium ≈ comparison, Hard ≈ design/debug/scenario).
- Row counts are not level-balanced within a subject: the generated cells were filled to a fixed target, not to proportional depth.

**Impact:** "Hard" is not calibrated across subjects, so difficulty-balanced sampling is possible but not difficulty-comparable.

**Mitigation:** do not use difficulty as a difficulty score; treat it as a coarse category. Re-annotate with a rubric if it drives training or evaluation.

## 8. Coverage is structurally complete but shallow in two places

The 5 original gaps were closed with generated questions; Machine Translation (Gwen) was filled from empty. There are now **no empty subject × difficulty cells**.

**Impact:** the matrix being full does not mean the cells are well sampled. Two flagged subjects are thin overall, and every gap-filling cell holds only the 5–10 generated questions, so per-cell counts inside small subjects remain very low.

## 9. Answer quality is unverified

The answers are machine-generated and were not fact-checked.

- No ground-truth answer, rubric or human rating exists anywhere in the file.
- **21,145 answers (88.3%) open with a sentence that appears verbatim in another answer.**
  There are about six stock openings, each covering roughly 2,500 rows ("Before choosing a
  direction I would make the trade-off explicit..."). The bodies after them differ - the
  largest opening group has 2,581 rows and 54 distinct continuations - so this is opening
  boilerplate, not duplicated answers, and no existing check measured it: the `TEMPLATES`
  list only matches whole-answer boilerplate (799 rows) and exact-duplicate counting sees
  1,086 rows.
- **799 answers (3.3%) are still boilerplate** — the templated "A good answer should address the requirements…" / "X can be understood as a core concept…" text. These are being replaced in batches of ~220 per day, limited by the free-tier token budget. Until they are gone, any answer-length or answer-quality statistic is contaminated by them.
- A mechanical cleanup (`clean_answers.py`) removed assistant chatter ("Let me know if you need anything else…") from 553 answers, leading filler ("Certainly.", "Sure.") and stray markdown bold. 28 answers still contain mid-text chatter and 9 begin with filler, because neither could be removed without damaging the sentence.
- **74 answers are under 100 characters** and 34 were too thin to clean; they are placeholders rather than explanations.
- 22,415 answers (93.6%) are written in first person and contain **invented personal and project details** (named publications, employer specifics). These are plausible-sounding but fabricated and must not be presented as real facts.
- A measurable subset of those: **10 answers state a numeric credential or tenure** ("I scored 860 on the TOEFL", "N years of experience"). `audit_dataset.py` reports these as a warning rather than a blocker, because a bare number with no unit cannot be judged with confidence and some questions supply it themselves. Treat the 10 as a floor, not a total: prose claims without a digit are invisible to the check.
- **108 answers stated verifiable-looking specifics** — a metric (`4.3x`, `60%`, `40 ms`) or a real company name (Google, Netflix…). These were the most misleading rows in the file. As of 15 November 2026, **100 have been rewritten** to describe mechanism and trade-off instead of results, and **8 were kept unchanged** because the question itself supplies the term ("How is Magento connected with Google Analytics?" must be allowed to answer *Google Analytics*). A measured re-audit finds **0 remaining**. The guard in `regenerate_answers.py --only-suspicious` rejects a rewrite that still contains a figure the question did not supply; both it and `audit_dataset.py` are question-aware.
- **1,086 rows share their answer text with another row** (167 distinct answer texts), and 1,253 are rewrite targets. `--apply` refuses any row whose rewrite invents a figure, so run files made before a guard existed cannot leak one in. Where two different questions were answered identically, the answer is generic rather than specific. `regenerate_answers.py` without `--only-template` targets these.

**Impact:** the file is suitable as a question bank and as style-consistent generation data; it is **not** suitable as a factual reference, a source of ground truth, or evidence of anyone's real experience.

## 10. Duplicate removal is textual only

Questions were compared after lowercasing, whitespace collapsing and trailing-punctuation stripping; generated candidates were additionally checked against the corpus by embedding cosine similarity > 0.90.

**Impact:** paraphrases of the same question ("What is X?" vs "Can you explain X?") remain as separate rows. 0 exact duplicates exist, but semantic duplication persists below that threshold and is not measured exhaustively.

**Mitigation:** run embedding-based clustering before treating rows as independent samples.

## 11. Prompt column is templated, not independent

- 23,954 rows carry 21,091 distinct prompts.
- Nearly all follow a few templates: `Prepare me for an interview on {subject}`, `You are a senior professional in the {subject} domain…`, `Interview questions about {subject}`.
- The prompt is therefore almost a function of the subject and is not an independent experimental variable.

**Impact:** prompt-level analysis, prompt-diversity claims or prompt ablation on this column are not meaningful.

## 12. Uneven granularity

Industry-subject answers are frequently long multi-paragraph scenarios (1,500–2,000 characters); small-subject answers are short definitional replies (150–300 characters).

**Impact:** length-based statistics, truncation thresholds and token budgets are dominated by subject, not by question difficulty. Any model trained here will associate subject with answer length.

## 13. Missing metadata

The file carries no information about:

- source documents, licences or consent for the underlying material;
- generation model, prompt version or temperature per row — only in aggregate, via this document and the generation scripts;
- language (assumed English throughout);
- author/reviewer, or any human sign-off;
- timestamps, so rows cannot be ordered or traced.

**Impact:** the dataset cannot be audited or safely redistributed without additional provenance work.

## 14. Intended use

**Reasonable:** a question bank for interview practice, a seed set for synthetic data generation, retrieval corpora for a study tool, and exploratory analysis of coverage.

**Not appropriate:** factual question answering, measuring real candidate ability, benchmarking without per-subject sample sizes, or any claim that the answers reflect verified expertise.

---

### Reporting checklist

When quoting this dataset, state at minimum: row count (23,954), that answers are machine-generated and unverified, that one answer per question is retained, that 799 answers (3.3%) are still templated, that 12 subjects hold 85.4% of rows, and that 2 subjects carry the `limited data` flag.
